Dataset Source & Adaptation Note

The original UCI Credit Card Default Dataset (published by I-Cheng Yeh, 2009 — Taiwan banking data) is one of the most widely used open datasets in BFSI analytics globally and is freely available on Kaggle and the UCI ML Repository. For this BA project, the dataset was extended with India-specific fields: city_tier, product_type, early_warning_flag, and collections_contact_made — simulated using Python (Faker + Pandas) with realistic Indian credit card market distributions. The business scenario is modelled on publicly available RBI regulatory reports and HDFC Bank annual disclosures on NPA trends. All findings are based on this simulation and are clearly labelled as such.

Business Problem & Objective
HDFC Bank's credit card division has seen its default rate climb from 15% to 22% over six consecutive quarters — a 47% relative increase that puts ₹480 crore annually in non-performing asset (NPA) risk. Internal audit identified that the collections team is contacting high-risk customers too late in the delinquency cycle and that the early-warning scoring model flags at-risk accounts but the output is not being consumed by collections workflows. The result: accounts that could have been recovered with early outreach are becoming full defaults by the time collections agents engage them.
Project Objective

Conduct a full BA analysis of the credit card default lifecycle — from early-warning trigger to collections resolution — identify process and system failures contributing to rising default rates, and produce a prioritised improvement roadmap with measurable KPI targets and a risk register aligned to RBI NPA guidelines.

Scope & Assumptions

In Scope

  • Early-warning model output → collections team action workflow
  • Collections contact strategy (timing, channel, priority)
  • Default resolution and write-off process

Out of Scope

  • Credit card underwriting and limit-setting process
  • Fraud detection and chargeback workflows
  • Machine learning model retraining (ML team scope)

Root Cause Analysis 5-Whys Method
Problem: Credit card default rate has risen from 15% to 22% over 6 quarters — ₹480 Cr NPA exposure
W1
Why is the default rate rising when the early-warning model is flagging at-risk accounts?
→ Because flagged accounts are not being contacted by collections agents within the critical 30-day window. Data shows a median of 47 days between flag and first contact — 17 days beyond the recovery window.
W2
Why are collections agents not acting on flagged accounts within 30 days?
→ Because the early-warning model output lives in a separate analytics dashboard (Tableau) that collections agents do not have access to. Agents work exclusively from the core banking CRM, which has no integration with the model's output.
W3
Why does the CRM not receive early-warning flags from the analytics system?
→ Because when the early-warning model was built (by the Data Science team 18 months ago), the requirement to push flags into the CRM was classified as "Phase 2" and never implemented. The model output was designed only for management reporting, not operational action.
W4
Why was the CRM integration classified as Phase 2 and never delivered?
→ Because the project was sponsored by the Analytics team, not the Collections team. The Collections team had no seat at the requirements table — so the operational workflow impact was never modelled. The project was declared complete at model go-live, without verifying that the output reached the people who needed to act on it.
RC
Root Cause: Missing cross-functional requirements ownership — the early-warning model was built without a BA bridging Analytics and Collections, leaving a critical last-mile gap between model output and agent action
Fix: (1) Integrate early-warning flags into the Collections CRM as a prioritised worklist — this is a system integration requirement, not a new ML requirement. (2) Define a collections contact SLA of 15 days from flag. (3) Establish a monthly model-to-outcome review to close the feedback loop. This is the single highest-ROI fix — estimated 8–12% default rate reduction in 12 months.

User Stories + MoSCoW Prioritisation 10 stories with full acceptance criteria
US-01 · Collections Agent Must Have
As a collections agent, I want to see all early-warning flagged accounts sorted by risk score in my CRM worklist so that I always work the highest-risk customers first.
Acceptance Criteria: Flagged accounts appear in CRM within 1 hour of nightly model run; list sorted descending by risk_score; each account shows: risk tier (High/Medium/Low), DPD days, outstanding balance, and SLA countdown. Refreshes on agent login.
US-02 · Collections Agent Must Have
As a collections agent, I want a 4-channel contact cascade so that I can reach high-risk customers even if they don't answer calls.
Acceptance Criteria: System auto-sends SMS after 48hrs no phone response; WhatsApp after 96hrs; email after 120hrs. Each channel attempt logged in CRM with timestamp. Agent notified when customer responds on any channel. "Unreachable" status only set after all 4 channels exhausted.
US-03 · Collections Team Lead Must Have
As a team lead, I want SLA countdown alerts for every flagged account so that I can intervene before a high-risk account breaches the 15-day contact SLA.
Acceptance Criteria: Alert fires on team lead dashboard when account reaches 80% of 15-day SLA (day 12). Alert shows agent name, account ID, risk score, and days remaining. Team lead can reassign account to another agent directly from alert screen.
US-04 · Collections Agent Must Have
As a collections agent, I want to log a customer's payment promise date in the CRM and have the system automatically remind the customer before the due date.
Acceptance Criteria: Agent can log promise_date and promise_amount in CRM from contact screen. Automated SMS reminder sent to customer at T-48hrs. If payment not received by T+1, account automatically escalates to next risk tier. Promise kept/broken tracked in CRM for pattern analysis.
US-05 · Head of Credit Risk Must Have
As the Head of Credit Risk, I want a real-time dashboard showing default rate trend, collections SLA compliance, and early-warning flag-to-contact conversion rate so that I can monitor the impact of the process improvement.
Acceptance Criteria: Dashboard shows: current default rate vs. 6-quarter trend, SLA compliance % (target ≥85%), flag-to-contact rate, avg days to first contact, and recovery rate by risk tier. Refreshes every 4 hours. Exportable to PDF for board reporting.
US-06 · Analytics Team Should Have
As an analytics team member, I want a monthly model-to-outcome report auto-generated so that I can track how accurately the early-warning model predicted actual defaults.
Acceptance Criteria: Report shows: predicted vs. actual defaults by risk tier, precision and recall by segment (credit limit band, product type, city tier), and false-positive rate. Generated by 5th of each month; shared to Collections and Risk leadership email distribution list.
US-07 · RBI Compliance Officer Should Have
As a compliance officer, I want a complete audit trail of all customer contact attempts and payment promise commitments so that we can demonstrate RBI-compliant collections conduct.
Acceptance Criteria: Every contact attempt logged: channel, timestamp, outcome, agent ID. Payment promises logged with amount, date, and result. All records retained for 7 years. Retrievable by customer ID or date range within 30 seconds.
US-08 · Collections Manager Should Have
As a collections manager, I want agent-level performance reports showing contact success rate, promise-kept rate, and recovery amount per agent so that I can coach underperforming team members.
Acceptance Criteria: Weekly agent scorecard: accounts worked, contact success %, promises obtained, promises kept %, INR recovered. Ranked view showing all agents. Drill-down to individual account level. Manager-only access.
US-09 · Collections Agent Could Have
As a collections agent, I want AI-suggested negotiation scripts based on the customer's risk profile and payment history so that I can have more effective conversations.
Acceptance Criteria: Script suggestions displayed as a collapsible panel on the contact screen; based on customer's DPD history, product type, and previous promise-kept rate. Agent can accept, modify, or dismiss. Suggestions not mandatory — remain optional.
US-10 · Customer (Cardholder) Could Have
As a customer, I want to be able to set up a self-service repayment plan via the HDFC Bank app so that I don't need to speak to a collections agent to restructure my outstanding balance.
Acceptance Criteria: Self-service repayment plan available in-app for customers in DPD30–DPD60 range; plan options: 3, 6, or 12 month EMI at no additional interest; plan acceptance triggers automatic removal from collections agent queue; collections notified of self-cure event.

SQL Analysis 15 queries against the 30,000-record dataset
Query 01 — Baseline Default Rate by Quarter
Track default rate trend across 6 quarters — confirm the 15% → 22% rise
SELECT
  quarter,
  COUNT(*) AS total_customers,
  SUM(default_next_month) AS defaults,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
GROUP BY quarter
ORDER BY quarter ASC;
-- Result: Q1=15.1% → Q2=16.8% → Q3=18.4% → Q4=19.9% → Q5=21.2% → Q6=22.0%
Query 02 — The Critical Gap: Flag-to-Contact Delay
How many days pass between early-warning flag and first collections contact?
SELECT
  AVG(days_since_last_contact) AS avg_days_to_contact,
  MEDIAN(days_since_last_contact) AS median_days_to_contact,
  COUNT(*) AS flagged_accounts,
  SUM(CASE WHEN days_since_last_contact > 30 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS missed_window_count,
  ROUND(missed_window_count * 100.0 / flagged_accounts, 2) AS missed_window_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
WHERE early_warning_flag = TRUE;
-- Result: avg 51 days | median 47 days | 63% of flagged accounts missed the 30-day window
Query 03 — Default Rate by Risk Tier vs. Contact Made
Does early contact actually reduce default rate? The business case for the fix.
SELECT
  risk_tier,
  collections_contact_made,
  COUNT(*) AS accounts,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
WHERE early_warning_flag = TRUE
GROUP BY risk_tier, collections_contact_made
ORDER BY risk_tier, collections_contact_made;
-- Key finding: High-risk + contacted = 28% default | High-risk + NOT contacted = 67% default
-- Contact makes a 39-percentage-point difference in the highest-risk segment
Query 04 — Default Rate by Product Type
Which card product has the worst default performance?
SELECT
  product_type,
  COUNT(*) AS total,
  ROUND(AVG(credit_limit), 0) AS avg_credit_limit_inr,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
GROUP BY product_type
ORDER BY default_rate_pct DESC;
-- Result: Fuel cards = 28.4% | Rewards cards = 21.1% | Travel cards = 18.9% | Platinum = 14.2%
Query 05 — Utilisation Rate vs. Default Correlation
Do high-utilisation customers default more? (Validates model feature importance)
SELECT
  CASE
    WHEN utilisation_rate < 0.5  THEN 'Low (<50%)'
    WHEN utilisation_rate < 0.8  THEN 'Medium (50–80%)'
    ELSE                               'High (>80%)'
  END AS utilisation_band,
  COUNT(*) AS customers,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
GROUP BY utilisation_band
ORDER BY default_rate_pct DESC;
Query 06 — Default by City Tier
SELECT
  city_tier,
  COUNT(*) AS customers,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month)
    *100.0/COUNT(*),2)
    AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
GROUP BY city_tier
ORDER BY
  default_rate_pct DESC;
-- Tier 3 cities: 31.2% default
-- vs Tier 1: 18.4%
Query 07 — Payment Pattern Analysis
SELECT
  SUM(CASE
    WHEN pay_status_t1 >= 2
    AND pay_status_t2 >= 2
    THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
    AS consecutive_delays,
  ROUND(SUM(default_next_month)
    *100.0/COUNT(*),2)
    AS default_rate_pct
FROM credit_card_customers
GROUP BY consecutive_delays;

Risk Register Implementation risks for the proposed process changes
High Risk
CRM–API integration delay
IT team estimates 12-week development cycle — every week of delay costs approximately ₹9 Cr in unrecovered defaults.
Mitigation: Run a manual daily export from Tableau to CRM as an interim bridge — reduced benefit but zero development required. Target: live within 4 weeks of project approval.
High Risk
RBI collections conduct breach
Multi-channel contact cascade must comply with RBI Fair Practices Code — no calls before 08:00 or after 19:00 IST; no harassment.
Mitigation: Legal and Compliance sign-off on channel rules before go-live. Build time-window restrictions into the automated cascade. Compliance officer named as stakeholder in RTM.
Medium Risk
Agent change resistance
Collections agents accustomed to FIFO queue may resist risk-score prioritisation — perceived as performance monitoring rather than workflow improvement.
Mitigation: Co-design the new worklist UI with 3 experienced agents as pilot testers. Frame as "work smarter, not harder" — risk-sorted queue reduces wasted calls on low-probability accounts.
Medium Risk
Model accuracy degradation
If the early-warning model drifts without recalibration, the risk-score queue will send agents to wrong accounts — eroding trust in the system.
Mitigation: Monthly model-to-outcome review (US-06) ensures drift is caught within 30 days. Define a precision floor of 60% — below this, revert to DPD-based prioritisation until model is retrained.
Medium Risk
WhatsApp / SMS delivery failure
Regulatory pre-approval needed for template messages on WhatsApp Business API — approval process can take 4–6 weeks.
Mitigation: Submit WhatsApp template approval as Week 1 action — parallel to development. SMS channel requires no pre-approval and serves as fallback during approval process.
Low Risk
Data quality in extended dataset
Simulation-extended fields (city_tier, product_type, contact flags) may not perfectly reflect HDFC's actual data distributions.
Mitigation: All simulation parameters and assumptions documented in the data dictionary. Findings framed as directional, not definitive — real implementation would begin with a data quality audit sprint.
